The money behind the Championship

What each of this season's 24 clubs earned, spent on wages and lost, taken from their latest accounts at Companies House. Most cover the 2024/25 season. Click any club for the full breakdown.

Wages as a share of revenue

Staff costs (wages, bonuses, social security and pensions) divided by revenue. The line marks 100%: clubs to the right of it spent more on staff than they brought in, before any other costs.

Where the money comes from

Revenue split into matchday (tickets and gate receipts), broadcasting (TV money, parachute and solidarity payments, prize money) and everything else (sponsorship, retail, catering, events).

Profit or loss before tax

Includes profits from selling players, which is how many clubs close the gap between what they earn and what they spend.

How this is put together

  • Source. Each club's most recent annual accounts covering the 2024/25 season, filed at Companies House (Bolton's are published by the club for its parent company). Figures are as reported, in pounds.
  • Which company. The club's main operating company, or the group that owns it where that's where the full accounts are filed. The company used is named on each club's page.
  • Different year ends. Most clubs' years end on 30 June; some use 31 May or 31 July. All cover a single 12-month season unless noted.
  • Revenue categories. Clubs split revenue differently. Their lines are mapped to matchday, broadcasting and commercial & other; anything that doesn't fit cleanly (e.g. catering) goes into commercial & other.
  • Checking the numbers. The filings are scanned images, so they're read with OCR (text recognition). Every revenue breakdown is checked to add up to the reported total, and staff costs and the result before tax are checked against the income statement.
  • Not a spending-rules (PSR) calculation. Clubs can exclude spending on academies, women's football, community work and infrastructure from the EFL's profit and sustainability limits, and those figures aren't fully disclosed. A large loss here doesn't by itself mean a club broke the rules.
